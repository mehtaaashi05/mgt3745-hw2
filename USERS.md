# User research and jobs

## Interview synthesis

### INT-01
Participant code / relevant circumstance / date: INT-01 / Manager oversees intern placements within Corporate & Institutional Banking Program / 09-02-2026

What happened and the participant's workaround: INT-01 described two contrasting examples from interns she had worked with in the past. In one, she described an intern who noticed he preferred the analytical work he saw credit analysts doing, built an informal connection on the credit team, asked questions, and sat in on meetings before eventually pursuing underwriting full-time. In the other, she described an intern who was similarly curious about credit but never reached out to anyone on the team directly; instead, she asked other interns informally and let the internship end without pursuing credit further.

What was reported, what you observed, and what you infer:
Reported: INT-01 said most managers "understand that interns are exploring their careers," and described the fear of looking disloyal as "more of a concern than something that necessarily happens." She also reported there is no formal process currently for an intern to "test out" another team.

Observed: In the examples INT-01 described, the interns who successfully explored another team did so by finding someone willing to have an informal conversation and exploring the team first, rather than immediately making a formal request to move.

Inferred: The framing of the initial conversation may affect how risky an intern perceives it to be. Asking "Can you tell me what your team does?" or "Will you walk me through what a typical day looks like for you?" appears less consequential than immediately saying "I want to move to credit." The informal approach allows the intern to learn about the team and determine whether the interest is genuine before pursuing anything formally.

Surprises:
- The reputational risk appeared to be more perceived than enforced; INT-01 said managers generally understand that interns are exploring their careers.
- The interns who successfully explored another team generally started with informal conversations rather than immediately requesting a transfer.
- There was no formal process for an intern to "test out" another team, even though interns may want to explore different career paths during an internship.

Evidence that confirms, challenges, or leaves the framing uncertain:
The interview confirms that interns can perceive reputational risk when expressing interest in another team. It challenges the assumption that this risk is necessarily materially enforced by managers. INT-01's examples suggest that the risk may be primarily perceived rather than an actual consequence imposed by managers. 

### INT-02
Participant code / relevant circumstance / date: INT-02 / Intern in Treasury Management who realized he wants to pursue Business Credit / 09/03/2026

What happened and the participant's workaround: INT-02 became curious about Business Credit a few weeks into the internship after conversations with interns in Business Credit. He did not act on the curiosity right away. His workaround was to start reaching out to first-year analysts on the team to learn more about the work and informally shadow them. Doing this confirmed his interest in Business Credit before he decided to try to switch teams officially.

What was reported, what you observed, and what you infer:
Reported: INT-02 said he hesitated because he did not want his current Treasury Management Manager to think he was unhappy and worried it could affect his return offer. He also reported that once he actually raised it, the reaction was "pretty positive," and stated directly that "I was worried about the risk for nothing."

Observed: INT-02's experience showed a gap between the risk he anticipated before raising the issue and the reaction he actually received after raising it. His initial hesitation delayed his exploration, but once he communicated his interest to his current team, he received support rather than the negative reaction he had feared.

Inferred: What INT-02 wanted initially was not to switch teams, but to answer whether he actually liked the work before committing to anything formal. Additionally, once he realized he wanted to switch, he wanted to make sure that his team was supportive of that decision.

Surprises:
- INT-02 delayed acting on his interest for weeks because of a risk that, based on his later experience, did not materialize.
- He was able to learn about the work through informal outreach and shadowing before making a formal request to switch teams.
- The reaction from his current team was "pretty positive," which directly challenged his initial expectation that expressing interest elsewhere could hurt his return offer.

Evidence that confirms, challenges, or leaves the framing uncertain:
The interview confirms that fear of appearing disloyal delayed action for weeks. It challenges the framing's assumption that the reputational risk is necessarily real or materially enforced. INT-02's experience suggests that the perceived risk can be greater than the actual reaction from the current team.

## Two job statements
JOB-01: When I curious about a team I am not placed on, I want to learn what the work is actually like without it reading as a request to leave my current team, so I can decide whether the interest is real before making it a formal conversation. Evidence: INT-01 and INT-02
JOB-02: When I want to learn about a team I'm curious about, I want to find someone there who is willing to talk with me, so I do not have to depend on already knowing the right person or getting lucky. Evidence: INT-02.

## Two user profiles
PROFILE-01: The Hesitant Explorer: An intern placed on one team who becomes curious about another within the first few weeks and has not yet built a connection into that team (known). Needs a way to learn what the work is like without the request reading as a signal of dissatisfaction with their current team (known). A constraint is that the intern is currently limited to asking peers, which yields secondhand and incomplete information (assumed). Whether the hesitation window is a couple of weeks or the whole internship is inferred, not established by either interview.

PROFILE-02: The Proactive Outreacher: An intern who becomes curious about another team and reaches out directly to junior team members there to ask questions and shadow informally, without any prior personal connection to those people (known). Needs to validate interest in the team through firsthand exposure before formally pursuing a move, and does not wait for someone else to initiate the process for him (known). A constraint is that this still depends on being willing to reach out cold and on the receiver being receptive to helping an unfamiliar intern. INT-02's outreach worked despite having no prior connection to the analysts he contacted (known). Whether cold outreach succeeds as reliably with more senior contacts, or outside corporate banking, is inferred, not established. 

Two exploratory interviews do not establish population prevalence. The patterns above describe what happened in these two cases. Broader claims would need more interviews.
